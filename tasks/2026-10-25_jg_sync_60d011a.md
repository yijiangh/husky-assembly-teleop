## 3. Where our code goes

### 3.1 Decisions

- **The 12 planning modules go to a new `shared/assembly_planning/`**, not `shared/planner/`.
  - His `planner/` is "base classes and helpers for the planner plugins", with "! No collision world here".
  - Ours hold the assembly's planning: routines, operator steps, planning scenes, IK, collision checks on a mirror
    pool, and the tamp route.
  - Keeping `shared/planning/` next to his `shared/planner/` would be a trap.
  - Module names stay as they are (least churn); renames are optional later.
- **Contracts:** three of our plugins offer something, so each gets a contract file (§3.3). `assembly_steps` offers
  nothing.
- **`shared/runs.py` → `shared/runs/runs.py`.** A one-module subfolder, like his `base_collision/`. It cannot stay at
  the top of `shared/`, and the contract imports its types.
- **`LoadedProcess`/`Step` are kept as our own types**, ported from 888854a into
  `shared/assembly_planning/process_steps.py` and built from the core `Process` the process plugin now serves.
  - They are used in about 20 of our files and tests, so only import lines change.
  - Swapping to the core's types (`joints_at`, `find_movement`) is a later, optional step (§5, S8).
- **His `scene_bodies.py` and `colors.py` go from `plugins/process/` to a new `shared/process_scene/`.** Both the
  process plugin and our planning scene (`scene_for`) need them. His own rule: what two plugins need lives in `shared/`.
- **`run_plan`, `run_ik` and `run_collisions` move into the motion_planner plugin** (`plugins/motion_planner/work.py`).
  - They build the contract's `CollisionReport`, so they cannot stay in a subfolder.
  - Only that plugin runs them.

**S1. Free drive under the lock (NEW paths; must be in the merge commit).** His 1d3e2c0 adds "requests":
`ArmInterface.request_free_drive()` keeps free drive on for 0.25 s. Without requests, the controller is stiff and free
drive ends within about 1.25 s. robot_control requests every tick (his `plugin.py:338`) while a laptop or phone
free-drive button is held, or Keep on is ticked. Rule in `_update_free_drive`, per arm, while `_held_by_other(...)`
says any robot is held:
- Do **not** call `request_free_drive()`.
- **Untick Keep on.** Otherwise free drive comes back by itself the moment the automatic motion ends.
- Switch the arm back to the joint controller if it is in free drive: `arm.hold()`, which now does that switch, or
  ours for `_free_driving`.
- Log once per lease (`_refusals_said`).

A Free controller started from the laptop's controller panel is already refused through `allowed` (#11d). This keeps
24db711's promise ("a robot taken ends free drive").

**S2. The safety sync at the executor (seam fix, after the merge).**
- What it is: "the safety sync" is the onboard `multi_arm_safety_sync`. `ArmInterface.safety_problem()` (his
  `arm.py:526`) is None only when all of these hold:
  - its status is at most 3 s old;
  - a dashboard is reached, or the arm is announced as simulated;
  - the arm is operational;
  - its program plays (not after Stop all until Resume).
- "A request" is `request_free_drive()` (S1).
- The arm already refuses every trajectory without the sync, in `_check_trajectory` and so in our dry run too, and
  `send_motion` turns that into REFUSED ("the log says why").
- Add `arm.safety_problem()` for every arm of the acting robot to movement_exec's `world_problem` for motion parts
  (`plugins/movement_exec/sequences.py:132`, after the `is_live` check). It runs before the prompt and again after
  Confirm, and returns `"<who> <arm>: <problem>"`.
- Then the operator is not asked to Confirm a motion the arm will refuse, and the line says why ("press Resume").
- Tests:
  - stale sync → refused before the prompt;
  - program stopped at the prompt → refused after Confirm, nothing published.
